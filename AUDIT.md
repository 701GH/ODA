# Audit Protocol and Findings

Extended documentation for the revision released in this repository. `README.md` describes the data; this file records how the audit was carried out and what it found.

## 1. The benchmark

RAGNet-3DOI is the 3DOI subset of RAGNet: 1,013 image–instruction pairs, each with a natural language instruction, an image and the sample's ground-truth mask. The subset covers **52 object categories**. The instructions were generated by GPT-4 from the 3DOI interaction-region annotations and were released without manual validation.

RAGNet also provides a textual answer for each sample. That field takes no part in this evaluation — what is compared here is a predicted mask against the ground-truth mask — so the revision keeps only the instruction, the image and the mask.

### Mask properties

Ground-truth masks are single-channel grayscale, and their original resolutions are not uniform:

| Resolution | Masks |
| --- | --- |
| 1920×1080 | 682 |
| 1280×720 | 218 |
| 640×368 | 10 |
| 480×368 | 1 |
| 1280×640 | 1 |

Masks are compared at each sample's own size.

The masks are not all stored in the same format: `images/` and `masks/` each contain **912 `.jpg` and 101 `.png`** files. The 101 PNG masks are strictly binary (only 0 and 255). The 912 JPEG masks carry a thin compression ring around the mask boundary: across all 1,013 masks, only 18 distinct pixel values occur — 0, the values 1–9, and the values 246–255 — with nothing in between.

This makes binarization well behaved but not arbitrary. Any threshold from **10 to 245** produces exactly the same foreground; a threshold of 1–9 instead counts the dark side of the compression ring as foreground and changes the result (median IoU 0.93, worst 0.66, against the threshold-invariant version). On small masks the ring is a large share of the area — the most affected mask has 741 pixels, 255 of which are ring. We binarize at 128.

Paths should be read from the JSONL files rather than constructed from the sample id, because of the mixed extensions.

## 2. Why the audit was done

The audit grew out of an observation made during evaluation; the data was not suspected beforehand. A pipeline of this kind has to fix an explicit interaction target before it can localize anything — which object, and which part of it. On the original set, the instruction and the mask frequently disagree about that target.

Such errors cannot be absorbed on the model side. If the task a model is asked to complete is not the target it is scored against, the score carries no information about the method.

## 3. Protocol

All 1,013 image–instruction pairs were read item by item and re-judged for whether the instruction matches its mask, with one of three verdicts: **keep**, **edit** or **reject**.

- All 1,013 items were read in **two full passes by a single annotator**. The first pass gave a verdict for every item; the second pass re-read all of them; items whose two verdicts disagreed were adjudicated once more.
- Reading relied only on the image, the instruction and the ground-truth mask. **No model prediction was consulted**, so the verdicts are independent of the method being evaluated.
- Because both passes came from the same person, **no inter-annotator agreement coefficient is reported**.
- The released record gives each item's final verdict, the instructions before and after revision, and the reason for the decision, but **does not distinguish the two passes**.

The matching criterion: an instruction matches its mask when, following that instruction, the region that needs to be touched is exactly the region the mask labels. If not, the instruction is edited; if the mask itself cannot express the structure of that part, the sample is rejected.

## 4. What the audit found

Three categories account for the problems found.

### 4.1 Granularity mismatch

The instruction asks about the whole object while the mask labels only one part of it. The mismatch shows up directly as a change of verb: the original instruction uses a *finding* verb (`find`, `locate`, `show`), which only asks where the object is; the revision switches to a *handing* verb (`hand`, `pass`, `bring`), which asks for the part that can be grasped and handed over. **68 edits were classified into this category during the audit.**

Two examples:

| id | original instruction | revised instruction | what the mask labels |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 104 | "Find a knife, please." | "Hand me a knife, please." | only the knife handle |
| 417 | "Show me where the wok is located." | "Could you bring me a wok?" | only the wok handle |

On such samples a model follows the original instruction literally, localizes the whole object, and lands far from the ground-truth mask.

### 4.2 Object mismatch

The instruction describes one object while the mask corresponds to a different one.

| id | original instruction | revised instruction | what the mask corresponds to |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 816 | "Can you fill this jug with water?" | "Pass me the kettle." | a kettle |
| 619 | "Can you bring me a vase, please?" | "Can you bring me a flower pot, please?" | a plant and its flower pot |

A few items go further and invert the action itself: for id 568 the original instruction is "Can you open the door for me?" while the mask corresponds to closing the door. It was revised to "Can you close the door for me?".

### 4.3 Defective mask

Here the instruction itself is fine; the mask is what fails, because it cannot correctly express the structure of the part at the target resolution. Two examples:

- **id 712** — "Please find the scissors." The hole in the scissors handle is too small to survive, so the mask fills it in and merges the two handles into one solid region.
- **id 380** — "Let's hang a picture frame on the wall." The mask covers the content of the picture on the wall, not the frame itself.

Items in this category are rejected without exception.

## 5. Why the masks were not corrected

We rejected these samples rather than redrawing their masks, because pixel-wise correction has no executable acceptance criterion in this task. How far a part boundary extends, and whether a small hole is kept or filled, both change the denominator of the intersection-over-union directly — and whether a mask we redraw is more accurate than the original cannot be judged objectively. A redraw that no independent check validates would introduce a new and hard-to-notice class of error.

The revision therefore changes only the instruction text. Images and masks are kept exactly as they are, so any score difference between the two versions can only come from the instructions. The cost is a smaller set of usable samples.

## 6. The same problem elsewhere

The three categories also occur outside RAGNet-3DOI. We ran a sampling check on the easy and hard splits of RAGNet-HANDAL and on ReasonAff, and observed the same kind of disagreement between instruction and ground-truth mask. That check did not record a systematic sample size or sampling method, so it is reported only as a clue that the phenomenon is not an isolated one; no claim of generality is made on its basis.

Those sets also contain a further difficulty this audit did not address: some of their instructions are themselves ambiguous, in the sense that no single correct region is determined.

RAGNet-3DOI was chosen for the full audit on grounds of scale. With 1,013 image–instruction pairs, an item-by-item reading is feasible; the other subsets are larger, and a full reading of them would exceed the effort available here.

## 7. Statistics

Every one of the 1,013 samples received one of the three verdicts:

| Verdict | Samples | Share |
| --- | --- | --- |
| keep | 616 | 60.8% |
| edit | 381 | 37.6% |
| reject | 16 | 1.6% |

997 items are released. **221 of the 381 edits (58%) remove a content word from the original instruction** — these change the target the instruction refers to, rather than only rephrasing it. A proportion that high indicates the mismatch is systematic rather than a scattering of typos.

One of the 16 rejects is **id 509**, whose image file `images/0509.jpg` is 0 bytes and unreadable in the original release. It still went through the audit, because its instruction and its mask are readable and the verdict rests on those; it is rejected and skipped during evaluation. This is also why the original version evaluates 1,012 of its 1,013 samples.

## 8. Limits of this release

- Only the instruction text is revised. Images, masks, splits and sample ids are unchanged.
- No inter-annotator agreement coefficient is reported: both passes were made by the same annotator.
- The per-category tallies in §4 are manual classifications made during the audit. They are not fields in the released data, and a reader cannot reconstruct them from `review_log.jsonl` alone.
- The other evaluation sets were sampled, not audited, and no generality is claimed for them.
- The 0-byte image `images/0509.jpg` is left as it is; it is an upstream defect, and replacing the file would change the data.
